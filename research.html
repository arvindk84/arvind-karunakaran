Research

Authority and accountability at work

Research on authority and accountability in the workplace, professions, organization theory, and technological and organizational change.

Current focus

My research examines authority and accountability in the workplace, especially in the context of technological change. I am currently focused on human–AI augmentation in the workplace: how emerging AI systems reshape the authority of professionals, the accountability of organizations, and the distribution of skill and opportunity at work.

Approach

I use ethnographic and field-based methods, participant observation and interviews, as my primary lens, complemented by comparative-historical analysis of archival data and computational analysis of large text corpora. Combining these approaches lets me trace how work is actually done on the ground while situating those practices within longer historical arcs and larger populations of cases.

Areas

Fields I work in

01

Sociology of work, occupations, and professions

How occupational groups claim, contest, and lose authority; how the boundaries between professions are drawn and redrawn.

02

Organization theory

Accountability, coordination, and control inside and across organizations, including meso-level dynamics between individuals, groups, and institutions.

03

Technological and organizational change

How new technologies (platforms, social media, and AI) reconfigure work, authority, and accountability, and how organizations respond.

Topics

Questions I study

Authority in the workplace

The bases of workplace authority and how status asymmetries between professions shape collaboration and conflict.

Professional, organizational, and algorithmic accountability

How accountability is produced, obscured, and redistributed as evaluation moves from institutions to crowds and algorithms.

Human–AI augmentation and reskilling

How AI systems augment (or displace) professional judgment, and the reskilling that augmentation demands.

Social media scrutiny

How public, crowd-based scrutiny reconfigures the accountability of frontline professionals and the organizations that employ them.

Selected themes in recent work

Several recent papers develop the idea of obscured accountability: the ways in which social-media scrutiny and platform intermediation can diffuse responsibility rather than concentrate it (ASQ 2024; Organization Science 2022). A parallel line of work examines status–authority asymmetry between adjacent professions (ASQ 2022), and a growing body of collaborative work maps the impact of AI on workplace inequality and the governance of platform power (Academy of Management Annals 2024, 2025).
